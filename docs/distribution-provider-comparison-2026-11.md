## What the trade-offs suggest

The commercial models fall into three broad patterns: annual subscriptions (DistroKid, TuneCore Unlimited, Ditto, UnitedMasters, Amuse), per-release pricing (CD Baby and TuneCore pay-per-release, with RouteNote Premium renewals), and no-upfront-cost revenue shares (RouteNote Free and ONErpm Emerging). These models shift risk differently: subscriptions can suit frequent releases but depend on continued renewal and plan rules; per-release pricing avoids a blanket annual plan but charges each release; revenue-share plans avoid upfront distribution charges while reducing artist receipts. “100%” claims are not interchangeable: read whether the base is gross or net, whether it covers recordings versus social/video monetization, and whether withdrawal, tax, currency, partner or optional-service deductions apply.

Release language also needs care. “Unlimited” is a provider’s published plan description, not proof of unlimited file storage, unrestricted use, or absence of review and policy enforcement. Ditto’s own terms expressly qualify fair use; RouteNote publishes concrete per-release track and file-size limits. Artist slots, annual renewals, plan eligibility and optional extras can matter as much as the headline upload claim.

YouTube Music delivery, Topic-channel generation, OAC linking, and Content ID are separate capabilities. Art Tracks may appear on an automatically generated Topic channel; OAC processes require eligibility and platform approval or mapping; Content ID matches eligible sound recordings in user-generated videos and has distinct rights, fees and revenue rules. Providers describe different levels of control and support, and channel mapping may take time. OAC wording should therefore be treated as a workflow or application claim—not as a guarantee of timing, approval, or permanent channel behavior.

Store-network counts (for example, “150+”) are provider marketing or directory claims, not evidence every release reaches every service or territory. Store selection, release eligibility, territorial availability, downstream partners and platform acceptance vary. In particular, the official RouteNote, ONErpm and Amuse pages reviewed do not establish a universal all-platform/all-territory guarantee.

## What this research does **not** prove about MONTYX

This is a comparison of the cited providers’ public claims and help materials as accessed on **2026-11-01**. It does not establish that MONTYX currently distributes music, has a direct DSP relationship or API, possesses delivery or accounting infrastructure, supports splits or payouts, meets any rights/compliance requirement, has YouTube integrations, or can obtain OAC approvals. It does not validate provider reliability, actual service levels, realized payout outcomes, or the completeness of any provider’s marketing claims. Silence in accessed pages is recorded as “not stated,” not as proof a capability or restriction does not exist. Prices and terms can change; confirm them with providers before making commercial commitments.

## Provider-page failures / evidence gaps

No provider failure was reported in the supplied research results. Several claims remain explicitly unestablished in the accessed official material: ONErpm Topic/OAC support and distinct Content ID terms; CD Baby’s current OAC request service (which its page says is paused since March 2025); Amuse’s specific manual Topic-to-personal-channel merge workflow; and a single universal payout cadence or minimum for several providers. These gaps are not filled by inference.

## Practical implication for the redesign

**An upstream DSP partner/API is required before MONTYX can promise music delivery or Topic-to-Official-Artist-Channel merging.** Any MONTYX-facing release status, pricing, royalty, payout, store coverage or YouTube workflow should be presented only after the upstream contract, API behavior, eligibility rules, territory/store coverage, rights workflow, support responsibilities and operational guarantees have been verified. YouTube delivery, OAC linking and Content ID should be scoped and described as separate functions. Use competitors’ public pages as research—not as a template: **do not copy DistroKid’s exact UI, wording, visual identity or trade dress.**


## MONTYX white-label context: S-Music Distribution

MONTYX’s owner identifies S-Music Distribution as the upstream white-label provider and confirms that MONTYX’s current customer-facing offer is free with no release-count cap. S-Music’s public homepage, accessed 2026-11-01, advertises free user accounts and white-label access, unlimited artist/user/catalog management, delivery to 375+ audio DSPs and 125+ video platforms. These are provider marketing claims, not independent proof that every release reaches every service or territory, and not proof of a blanket platform verification badge.

The same S-Music homepage states that its white-label arrangement retains 20% of gross royalties; it says the white-label operator holds the remaining net royalties and rights. Thus, “free” describes the published account/upfront-cost offer, not a 100%-of-gross payout promise. MONTYX’s exact artist-facing agreement/split has not been independently verified. The page also expressly says Beatport and Traxsource were not available to third-party white-label accounts at its accessed publication state, with a future fix only hoped for. Do not advertise those services as currently available through MONTYX’s white-label route.

S-Music’s page advertises “YouTube OAC & Shorts” and says an “Official Artist topic Channel” can be provided under the label name; that wording does not clearly document a universal artist-owned personal-channel/Topic-channel merge workflow. YouTube’s own OAC help states that an OAC can bring together subscribers/content from an artist’s Topic channel, Vevo (if available), and existing channel, but eligibility and the OAC outcome belong to YouTube. Topic/Art Track delivery, OAC application/channel consolidation, and Content ID are separate features. MONTYX’s public portal Content ID guide separately requires exclusive worldwide rights, original/distinct content, no third-party claims or prior Content ID enrollment, and excludes AI-generated/AI-assisted musical elements.

## Interpretation notes for the public comparison

- Label owner-confirmed account terms (“free” and “no release-count cap”) as MONTYX’s offer, not an S-Music contractual guarantee. A store’s review, legal/rights eligibility, selected-store settings, territories, and platform policies still apply.
- Attribute the “375+ audio / 125+ video” counts to S-Music. Do not translate them into “every service” or claim every release is sent to all destinations.
- Attribute S-Music’s stated 20% gross share to the provider and distinguish it from MONTYX’s exact net payment obligation, which requires the current signed white-label/artist terms.
- Do not use Spotify/YouTube “verified” badges. No platform approval/certification credential for MONTYX was verified in this research.
- Describe OAC only as an application/YouTube-controlled consolidation process and distinguish it from Topic-channel Art Track delivery and Content ID matching. Do not promise a personal/Topic merge, approval or timeline.
